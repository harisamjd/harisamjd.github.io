# Creative Direction

## Portfolio Website — Haris A. Majid

**Version:** 1.0

---

# 1. Creative Vision

This website should feel like a premium digital product rather than a personal portfolio.

When visitors finish browsing, they should remember the experience, not the framework behind it.

The goal is not to impress visitors with animations.

The goal is to make them feel that every interaction has been carefully designed.

The website should quietly communicate craftsmanship.

---

# 2. Experience Statement

The website should feel like:

> "Walking through a modern product exhibition."

Not

> "Scrolling through someone's resume."

Every section should naturally invite visitors to continue exploring.

There should never be an abrupt transition.

---

Curiosity

Action

Visitors should never feel overwhelmed.

A dashboard.

A developer playground.

A marketing website.

---

# 7. Visual Mood

Imagine entering a quiet, beautifully designed exhibition.

Large open spaces.

Soft lighting.

Minimal distractions.

Every object has a purpose.

Nothing feels rushed.

The visitor controls the pace.

The website should create the same feeling.

# 9. Storytelling Principle

Visitors should gradually discover who I am.

Not receive all information at once.

Every section should answer exactly one question.

Example:

Who is Haris?

↓

How did he get here?

↓

What does he do?

↓

How does he think?

↓

What has he built?

↓

What impact has he created?

↓

How can I contact him?

---

# 10. Rhythm

The website should breathe.

Do not place large sections back-to-back without visual pauses.

Use whitespace intentionally.

Think of rhythm like music.

Some moments are energetic.

Some moments are quiet.

Alternate between both.

---

# 11. Whitespace Philosophy

Whitespace is content.

Whitespace is not empty space.

Whitespace creates focus.

Never fill space simply because it exists.

If something feels crowded, remove elements instead of shrinking them.

---

# 12. Typography Direction

Typography should carry the experience.

Large headlines.

Short paragraphs.

Comfortable line height.

Minimal emphasis.

Readable hierarchy.

The visitor should enjoy reading.

---

# 13. Photography Style

Use professional photography.

Natural lighting.

Neutral backgrounds.

No exaggerated poses.

No stock-photo feeling.

Authenticity over perfection.

---

# 14. Illustration Style

If illustrations are used:

Minimal.

Outline style.

Geometric.

Consistent stroke.

Never cartoonish.

---

# 15. Icon Style

Use one icon family only.

Icons should support navigation.

Never dominate content.

---

# 16. Motion Style

Motion should feel:

Natural.

Soft.

Controlled.

Confident.

Invisible.

Users should notice that the experience feels smooth.

Not that things are moving.

---

# 17. Scroll Philosophy

Scrolling is storytelling.

Each scroll should reveal new information.

Not simply move the page.

Avoid sections that feel disconnected.

Every scroll should reward curiosity.

---

# 18. Project Presentation Philosophy

Projects are stories.

Not screenshots.

Every project should answer:

Why did this project exist?

What was the business problem?

Why was it difficult?

How was it solved?

What changed afterwards?

Technology is introduced only after the problem is understood.

---

# 19. Business Before Technology

Visitors should remember:

The business value.

The measurable impact.

The process.

The thinking.

Technology is secondary.

Business outcomes are primary.

---

# 20. Consistency

Every page element should feel as though it belongs to the same product.

Buttons.

Cards.

Spacing.

Typography.

Animations.

Icons.

Everything should follow one visual language.

---

# 21. Microinteractions

Every interaction should provide subtle feedback.

Examples:

Buttons gently respond.

Navigation smoothly updates.

Cards slightly elevate.

Links transition elegantly.

Nothing should surprise the user.

---

# 22. Attention Management

The website should always answer:

Where should the visitor look next?

Use contrast.

Whitespace.

Typography.

Motion.

Never use multiple competing focal points.

---

# 23. Visual Hierarchy

Priority order:

Headlines

↓

Business Impact

↓

Projects

↓

Supporting Content

↓

Secondary Details

Visitors should understand hierarchy without thinking.

---

# 24. Reading Experience

Write less.

Communicate more.

Never create large walls of text.

Prefer:

Headlines.

Supporting paragraphs.

Visuals.

Metrics.

Diagrams.

Case studies.

---

# 25. Case Study Philosophy

Every project should feel like a consulting presentation.

Business Problem

↓

Research

↓

Analysis

↓

Decision

↓

Implementation

↓

Results

↓

Reflection

This aligns naturally with Business Analysis and Process Improvement roles.

---

# 26. Personal Branding

The website should consistently communicate these qualities:

Analytical.

Reliable.

Business-oriented.

Engineering mindset.

Continuous learner.

Problem solver.

Digital transformation advocate.

The visitor should infer these qualities from the experience, not just read them as claims.

---

# 27. Visual Do's

Use generous whitespace.

Use consistent spacing.

Use subtle animations.

Use high-quality typography.

Use meaningful imagery.

Use structured layouts.

Keep everything intentional.

---

# 28. Visual Don'ts

Do not use random gradients.

Do not use unnecessary 3D effects.

Do not use excessive glassmorphism.

Do not overload the interface.

Do not animate everything.

Do not use more than one accent color.

Do not sacrifice readability for visual effects.

---

# 29. Final Creative Principle

The highest compliment for this website should be:

"This feels like a product."

Not:

"This has cool animations."

Craftsmanship is invisible.

Visitors should remember the clarity, the story, and the professionalism.

The design should quietly demonstrate the same qualities I bring to every project:

Thoughtful analysis.

Structured problem solving.

Attention to detail.

Continuous improvement.

That is the experience this website should deliver.
